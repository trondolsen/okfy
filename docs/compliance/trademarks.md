# Why this note exists

A product name can be protected as a trademark. **okfy** is a non-commercial project. What matters, then, is simpler: that the name is free to use, and that no one else's earlier rights stand in the way of a small, non-commercial tool. This note records that check and explains what it found. It is written to be read straight through, it names no third party, and it is a record of checks — not legal advice.

The check follows the usual first step of looking a name up in the registers before adopting it.[^euipo-availability]

# The name

`okfy` is one lowercase word. It is invented rather than taken from a dictionary: it is the **OKF** acronym with a single letter added. It names a free, non-commercial tool for exploring knowledge bundles written in the Open Knowledge Format, with knowledge management and an optional AI assistant.[^readme]

A trademark is granted for particular goods and services, sorted into standard **classes**.[^nice] For a tool like this, three classes are in play:

- **Class 9** — software you download.
- **Class 42** — software offered as a service, hosting, and AI services.
- **Class 35** — data and information services, which sit alongside the other two.

# What was checked

The whole name was looked up in the official registers of the markets that matter — the EU, the US and Norway — and also in a service that searches many registers at once.[^euipo][^uspto][^patentstyret][^tmview] Because the name is the shorter word **`okf`** with a letter on the end, that shorter word was searched as well. The international registers are relevant because they show how a mark filed elsewhere can reach these markets.[^branddb][^madrid]

| Where | Searched | Result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| EU (EUIPO) | `okfy` | nothing |
| US (USPTO) | `okfy` | nothing |
| Norway (Patentstyret) | `okfy` | nothing |
| Many registers (TMview) | `okfy` | one exact name, in an unrelated field |
| Many registers (TMview) | `okf` | the shorter word is taken |

# What the checks mean

The whole name is free in the three markets that matter. No one has registered or applied for **`okfy`** for software, knowledge management or AI in the EU, the US or Norway.

One exact match does exist, but in a field far from software and unrelated to this tool, so it does not stand in the way. Several similar names that contain the string `okfy` do exist in classes 9, 35 and 42 — but only in registers outside these markets. None of them covers the EU, the UK, Norway or the US.

The one point worth knowing is about the shorter word underneath the name. The word **`okf`** is itself a registered trademark in the EU and the UK, in classes 9 and 42 — the same two classes as this tool. Because `okfy` is that word with a single letter added, the EU and the UK are the only markets here where the name sits close to an existing right. The US and Norway are clear for the same classes.

# What the check does not cover

- It is a snapshot. Registers change, so it describes only the state at the time of the search.
- It covers registered and pending marks in the registers listed. It does not cover use that is never registered — on products and packaging, in app stores, in package registries, in code hosting, or in domain names and social handles.
- It does not read the exact wording of the earlier registration, or any international extension of a mark filed outside these markets.

# What follows from this

In the US and Norway the name is free to use for the classes that matter. In the EU and the UK it sits close to an earlier registered word in the same classes; that does not stand in the way of a non-commercial tool, and it matters only if the project ever became commercial or sought a registration. Using the name openly, and without claiming any connection to the format or its makers, keeps the risk low. The checks are worth re-running before any change of that kind.
